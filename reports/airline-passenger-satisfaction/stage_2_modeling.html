02 · Objective & Research Questions

From descriptive analysis to predictive modelling

The purpose of Stage 2 was to build and evaluate models that classify whether a passenger is neutral or dissatisfied or satisfied, and to assess how much additional predictive information is available from service-experience ratings.

Research questions

  1. How accurately can passenger satisfaction be classified using passenger and flight information?
  2. How much does adding service-experience ratings improve predictive performance?
  3. Does a non-linear model (Random Forest) outperform a linear baseline (Logistic Regression)?
  4. Which input features does the best-performing model rely on most?

The target was encoded as 0 = neutral or dissatisfied and 1 = satisfied. The analysis is predictive and observational; it does not estimate causal effects.

Random state for split

The official Kaggle training set was divided into an 80% model-training subset and a 20% validation subset using stratification and random_state=42. The original Kaggle test set was kept separate until final evaluation.

Two feature sets

Model A — baseline feature set

Passenger and travel characteristics, flight information, and Wi-Fi information.

Includes gender, customer type, age, type of travel, class, flight distance, delays, Wi-Fi rating and Wi-Fi applicability.

Model B — expanded feature set

All Model A features plus 13 additional service-experience ratings.

Includes online boarding, check-in, baggage handling, seat comfort, inflight entertainment, food and drink, and other service ratings.

Feature-set clarification: Wi-Fi rating is itself a service-experience measure, but it was retained in Model A in the implemented workflow. Therefore, Model A is not a strictly pre-experience or service-rating-free model. Model B adds the other 13 service ratings.

Feature preparation

  • The passenger ID was excluded from modelling.
  • Wi-Fi value 0 represented “Not Applicable”; it was separated into wifi_rating_applicable and wifi_rating, with non-applicable ratings treated as missing.
  • Missing arrival-delay values were median-imputed, with a missingness indicator retained.
  • Imputation and encoding were fitted within scikit-learn pipelines to avoid fitting preprocessing on validation or test observations.
04 · Methodology

Models and evaluation strategy

Logistic Regression

Logistic Regression was used as the baseline classifier. Numerical variables were median-imputed and standardised; categorical variables were imputed and one-hot encoded with a reference category dropped.

Random Forest

Random Forest was used to capture non-linear patterns and feature interactions. Scaling was not required for tree-based models. Categorical variables were one-hot encoded without dropping a reference category.

Overfitting control

The initial Random Forest models fitted the training data almost perfectly, indicating overfitting. Constrained versions were trained with:

Evaluation metrics

  • Accuracy: overall proportion of correct classifications.
  • ROC-AUC: ability to rank positive cases above negative cases across classification thresholds.
  • Macro F1: unweighted average of the F1 scores for both classes.
  • Precision and recall: assessed separately for neutral/dissatisfied and satisfied passengers.
  • Confusion matrix: shows correct and incorrect classifications by class.

Interpretation

  • Type of Travel was the strongest Model B predictor, indicating that business versus personal travel carries substantial information for classification.
  • Wi-Fi rating and applicability were important in both models, showing that Wi-Fi-related information contributes strongly to predictions in this dataset.
  • Customer Type ranked highly, consistent with the Stage 1 finding that loyal and disloyal customers had different satisfaction rates.
  • Online boarding and check-in service were among the most important additional service ratings.
  • Flight delays and gender had very low individual permutation importance in these models.

Permutation importance measures predictive reliance, not direction of association or causation. Correlated or overlapping features can share predictive information, which may reduce the apparent individual importance of each feature. Small negative values can occur from evaluation variability and should be interpreted as approximately zero importance.

08 · Business Interpretation

Potential implications for an airline

The findings can inform how an airline segments passenger experience and analyses satisfaction. They should be treated as hypotheses and decision-support insights, not as proof that a particular intervention will cause satisfaction to improve.

Segment the passenger journey

Travel purpose and customer type were highly informative. An airline could examine whether business and personal travellers have different expectations and service priorities.

Monitor digital touchpoints

Wi-Fi, online boarding and check-in appeared among the important predictors. These are practical areas for more detailed service-quality monitoring.

Use service feedback to identify risk

Where survey ratings are available, a model like Model B could help flag responses associated with neutral or dissatisfied experiences for follow-up analysis.

Investigate delays in context

Delays had relatively low predictive importance compared with several experience and passenger variables. This does not mean delays are operationally unimportant; their effects may be context-dependent or captured partly by other variables.

Operational distinction: Model B is a post-experience classification model because it uses service ratings collected during or after the journey. It should not be presented as a pre-flight early-warning model. Model A also includes Wi-Fi rating and flight-delay information, so it is not strictly a pre-flight model either.
09 · Limitations

What this analysis does not establish

  1. Dataset transferability: the analysis uses a public Kaggle dataset, not Finnair or another airline's operational data. Results may not transfer to another carrier, route network or customer population.
  2. Post-experience features: Model B uses service ratings that may only be available after passengers have experienced the service. Its strong classification performance does not imply that satisfaction can be predicted before the journey.
  3. Association is not causation: feature importance does not show that changing a feature will cause satisfaction to change.
  4. Single held-out split: validation and test data are from the same source dataset and split structure. External or temporal validation would be needed to assess broader generalisation.
  5. Correlated predictors: permutation importance can understate the individual importance of correlated features because other variables may carry overlapping information.
  6. Model complexity: Random Forest is less directly interpretable than Logistic Regression. The feature-importance analysis offers a global view but does not explain every individual prediction.
  7. Classification threshold: reported class metrics use the classifier's default decision threshold. Different operational costs for false positives and false negatives could justify a separate threshold analysis.
10 · Conclusion & Next Steps

Stage 2 conclusion

Across the four evaluated model variants, Regularized Random Forest Model B achieved the strongest held-out test performance (95.95% accuracy, 0.9936 ROC-AUC and 0.9588 macro F1). The result was consistent with validation performance and substantially exceeded the corresponding Model A result.

The comparison indicates that the 13 additional service ratings provide substantial predictive information beyond the passenger, travel, flight and Wi-Fi features already included in Model A. The strongest Model B permutation-importance features were Type of Travel, Wi-Fi rating, Customer Type, Wi-Fi applicability and Online boarding.

Model selection: retain Regularized Random Forest B as the primary predictive candidate and Regularized Random Forest A as a comparison model for settings where the additional 13 service ratings are unavailable.
